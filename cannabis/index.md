2 · What the law says

### Ohio said yes in 2023, then rewrote the rules in 2026.

Voters passed Issue 2 with 57%. The legislature replaced it with Senate Bill 56, which took effect March 20, 2026 and now governs medical and adult use together.

1. Sep 2016 **Medical marijuana becomes legal** House Bill 523. The first medical sales come in January 2019.
2. Nov 2023 **Issue 2 passes, 57% to 43%** Adults 21 and over can buy, carry and grow at home.
3. Aug 2024 **Adult-use sales begin** At stores licensed for both medical and adult use.
4. Jun 2025 **The budget redirects the tax money** House Bill 96 replaces Issue 2's split. More below.
5. Mar 2026 **Senate Bill 56 takes effect** One law for medical and adult use. Extract cap falls to 70%. Intoxicating hemp moves into dispensaries.
6. Dec 11, 2026 **Federal hemp rule arrives** A new national definition of hemp, delayed one month by Congress.

2.5 oz · **Possession limit** for plant material, plus 15 grams of extract.

6 / 12 · **Home grow:** 6 plants per adult, 12 per home. Going over by any number is now a crime.

0.4 mg · **Hemp limit:** more THC than this per container, and it's legally marijuana, sold only in dispensaries.

400 · **Statewide cap** on dispensaries, at least a mile apart. 231 operate today.

Home · **Smoking and vaping** are allowed only on private residential or farm property.

**The details: what changed with Senate Bill 56**

**Structure.** SB 56 repealed the Issue 2 law (Chapter 3780) and merged both markets into Chapter 3796. Licenses are now dual-use.

**Potency.** 35% THC for plant material and 70% for extracts, for medical and adult use. Issue 2 had told regulators to set limits no lower than 35% and 90%. The state may raise the extract limit by rule. A Division of Cannabis Control guidance sheet dated March 2026 still lists 90% for manufactured products, so confirm with the Division before relying on one number.

**Hemp.** Since March 20, 2026, hemp means no more than 0.3% total THC, including THCA. A finished product with more than 0.4 mg of THC per container, or with cannabinoids made outside the plant (such as delta-8 converted from CBD), is not hemp. A plan to let bars and stores sell hemp drinks was vetoed. A federal court has blocked parts of the ban for ten plaintiff companies; the appeal is pending.

**Other changes.** Marijuana bought out of state is contraband. No new dispensary within a mile of another, or where liquor is sold. Stricter packaging and advertising. A path to expunge past minor possession offenses. Local governments can ban new operators but not home grow, and can't close stores licensed before March 20, 2026.

**Tax.** The 10% rate stayed. An earlier version of SB 56 would have raised it to 15%.

4 · What the research says

### The clearest risk is daily use of high-potency cannabis, especially for the young.

Di Forti et al., Lancet Psychiatry, 2019: 901 first-episode patients and 1,237 controls at 11 sites in Europe and Brazil. Gridlines every 1×.

**"Substantial"** The National Academies' word for the evidence linking cannabis and schizophrenia, highest among the most frequent users.

**15%** Share of schizophrenia among Danish men 16 to 49 that could be tied to cannabis use disorder in 2021, if the link is causal.

**11×** Risk of a psychotic disorder for teens who used cannabis in an Ontario study. No significant link for young adults.

**The arrow points both ways.** A genetic study found only a small causal effect of starting cannabis on schizophrenia, and stronger evidence that people predisposed to schizophrenia are more likely to use cannabis. Most "cases that could be prevented" figures assume the link is causal. People with a family history of psychosis show more sensitivity to cannabis, but no study has measured risk by product strength for them.

**More findings: dependence, withdrawal, vomiting, daily use**

**Potency and dependence.** A review of 20 studies found higher-potency use was linked to more psychosis and more cannabis use disorder; results for depression and anxiety were mixed.

**Tolerance.** In a Colorado study, people using concentrates had more than twice the THC in their blood as flower users but no more measured impairment, consistent with tolerance. That is not evidence that concentrates are harmless. Dab users report more tolerance and withdrawal.

**Withdrawal.** About 47% of regular or dependent users have cannabis withdrawal, from 17% in general surveys to 87% among inpatients.

**Severe vomiting (cannabis hyperemesis syndrome).** U.S. emergency visits coded for it rose sharply after a dedicated diagnosis code began in October 2025; the CDC says better coding may explain part of the jump. In Ontario, visits rose 13-fold from 2014 to 2021.

**Daily use.** In 2025, 21.4 million Americans used marijuana on 20 or more days in the past month, more than the 17.2 million who drank that often. In Ohio, 16% of people 12 and older used marijuana in the past month (2023 to 2024), and 28% of those 18 to 25.

5 · What other places tried

### Caps, potency taxes and small edibles. No one has measured the health results yet.

Ohio's caps are higher than Vermont's and Quebec's. Here is what other places did.

Ohio **35% · 70%** Flower and extract caps, the same for medical and adult use.

Vermont **30% · 60%** Flower and solid concentrates. Its own cannabis board called the evidence for the concentrate cap inconclusive.

Illinois **10% → 25%** A potency-tiered tax: 10% on flower up to 35% THC, 25% above it.

Canada **10 mg** The most THC allowed in a whole package of edibles. A 2024 review kept it and suggested taxes that rise with THC.

Quebec **30%** A cap on non-edible products. A 2026 study found less use of strong extracts, and more illegal buying. It can't isolate the cap's effect.

No real-world evaluation of how U.S. potency caps or potency taxes affect health exists yet.

**The tradeoff, and sources**

Caps and steep potency taxes may cut exposure to the strongest legal products, and they may push some buyers to illegal or out-of-state sellers. In a 2026 experiment with 1,525 U.S. buyers, potency-based taxes moved people away from the strongest products but didn't lower total THC use, and up to 89% of that shift could be offset by buying illegally. Massachusetts regulators found the evidence insufficient to recommend a cap in 2021, while noting greater risk for young people using high-THC products. RAND has proposed taxing THC content directly.
