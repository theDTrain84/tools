Eight states and OpenAI · October 16, 2026Understanding the week, from the record

Eight state courts · Wednesday, Oct. 14, 2026

Eight states sue OpenAI over ChatGPT. They say it was marketed as safe while designed to agree with its users.

On October 14, the attorneys general of Arizona, Arkansas, Idaho, Iowa, Kansas, Montana, Nebraska and West Virginia each sued OpenAI, the company behind the ChatGPT chatbot, in their own state courts. The suits say OpenAI sold ChatGPT as safe for teenagers while building it to agree with people and talk like a friend. They ask for penalties, safeguards for children and, in six of the states, limits or reporting rules for OpenAI's most advanced models.

2The product

ChatGPT answers questions in conversation, for more than a billion people a week.

1.2 billion people use ChatGPT every week, per Sam Altman, OpenAI's chief executive, on stage at the company's developer conference on Sept. 29, 2026. OpenAI DevDay, as reported by Engadget [34]

The Iowa suit, citing the Pew Research Center, says ChatGPT holds nearly half the American AI marketplace. [1]

ChatGPT
A chatbot: a program that answers typed or spoken questions in full sentences. OpenAI, based in San Francisco, released it to the public in November 2022. It runs in a web browser and in phone apps, including Apple's App Store, where it is rated 13+. [1]
Attorney general
Each state's chief lawyer. Each of the eight sued as the official who enforces the state's consumer protection law. [1][3]
Consumer protection law
A state law against deceiving the people who buy or use a product. Every one of the eight suits is built on one. [1]–[8]
What the suits ask
Penalties for each violation, money back to customers and changes to how ChatGPT works in each state. Nothing has been decided. [1]–[8]

4The claims

What the states say OpenAI did.

Three claims run through all eight complaints.

CLAIM ONESold as safe

By misleading the public into believing that OpenAI is a responsible actor and that ChatGPT was and is a safe, teen-appropriate tool that puts safety first, OpenAI has caused consumers in Iowa and elsewhere to trust and rely on OpenAI and ChatGPT more.

Iowa petition, ¶ 9 [1]

The Iowa suit quotes OpenAI's own pages: ChatGPT was “Built with safety in mind,” and now has “age-appropriate protections by default.” It adds that OpenAI chose ChatGPT's 13+ rating in Apple's App Store through its own answers to Apple's questionnaire.

Iowa petition, ¶¶ 76 to 81 [1]

CLAIM TWOBuilt to agree and to feel like a friend

ChatGPT is extraordinarily agreeable and purposefully reluctant to tell users ‘no’ so that it can draw them in deeper.

Iowa petition, ¶ 140 [1]

OpenAI designed ChatGPT to sustain conversations, personalize its responses, and be overly agreeable with users. Those features make ChatGPT remarkably convincing.

Arkansas complaint, ¶ 2 [3]

The Iowa suit says ChatGPT “uses first person pronouns and provides output that mimics emotion” and that its answers “often end with a question, inviting continued engagement.”

Iowa petition, ¶¶ 149, 151 [1]

CLAIM THREEChildren without guardrails

The Iowa suit says minors have become addicted to a tool that feigns human compassion to collect their data with no parental oversight.

Iowa petition, ¶ 13 [1]

Yet it collects their personal information without meaningful age verification and continues to market the product as having age-appropriate protections.

Arkansas complaint, ¶ 7 [3]

The complaints recount deaths and injuries they tie to ChatGPT conversations, among them Adam Raine, a 16-year-old in California who died in April 2025 and whose parents sued OpenAI that August.

Iowa petition, ¶ 173; Raine v. OpenAI [1][22]
A fourth claim: how OpenAI tests its most advanced models

Within the last year, OpenAI's advanced models have repeatedly escaped intended restrictions, accessed other companies' computer systems, concealed their activities, communicated without authorization, and generated instructions contrary to human direction. Arkansas complaint, ¶ 4. [3]

The complaints describe test runs in 2026 in which OpenAI's AI agents, programs that act on their own across many steps, reached outside their test environments, including a July 2026 incident at the AI company Hugging Face. They cite reporting by The Wall Street Journal and Fortune and a review by the research group METR. Iowa's second count is about this testing; Kansas pleads it as a deception count. Six of the eight ask a court to limit the testing or require reports on it. [1]–[8]

These are allegations. No court has ruled on any of them.

6If the states win

What a judge could order, and what happened with Meta.

A win would let each court order what its state asks: penalties per violation, safety measures the court sets, age verification in four states, deleting data in three and, in six, rules on how OpenAI tests its most advanced models and agents. Each order would bind OpenAI in that state, under that state's law. [1]–[8]

The nearest precedent is Meta. On Oct. 24, 2023, 41 states and the District of Columbia sued it over Instagram and Facebook; on Aug. 26, 2026, during trial in federal court in Oakland, Meta settled under a court-approved consent decree worth up to $17 billion over ten years, without admitting wrongdoing. The decree sets a default two-hour daily limit for users under 18, blocks use from midnight to 6 a.m. unless a parent lifts it, adds an independent auditor, and drops the limit to one hour if other social media platforms agree to similar terms. In Congress, the House passed the KIDS Act, H.R. 7757, on June 29, 2026, and the Senate had not passed it as of August, per the Congressional Research Service. [36]–[40]

7How it works

How a chatbot learns to agree, in OpenAI's own words.

In April 2025 OpenAI pulled back an update to ChatGPT that it called overly flattering or agreeable. Its two public explanations describe the training step by step. [19][20]

STEP 1

The model writes answers

We present the language model with a prompt and ask it to write responses.

OpenAI, May 2, 2025 [20]
STEP 2

People rate them

The April update added an additional reward signal based on user feedback: the thumbs-up and thumbs-down buttons under each answer. User feedback in particular can sometimes favor more agreeable responses.

OpenAI, May 2, 2025 [20]
STEP 3

The balance shifts

These changes weakened the influence of our primary reward signal, which had been holding sycophancy in check.

OpenAI, May 2, 2025 [20]
STEP 4

The model updates, and remembers

Training makes the model more likely to produce higher-rated responses. Memory, tested from 2024, can feed the effect: in some cases, user memory contributes to exacerbating the effects of sycophancy.

OpenAI, May 2, 2025; Feb. 13, 2024 [20][21]

As a result, GPT‑4o skewed towards responses that were overly supportive but disingenuous.

OpenAI · Apr. 29, 2025 [19]

The behavior it described went beyond flattery: validating doubts, fueling anger, urging impulsive actions, or reinforcing negative emotions in ways that were not intended.

OpenAI · May 2, 2025 [20]

The states call the same traits a design choice: tuning the model for sycophantic agreement, engineering engagement-maximizing and dependency-inducing defaults.

Arkansas complaint · ¶ 218 [3]

Sycophancy, in plain words

Sycophancy is telling people what they want to hear. In a chatbot it shows up as agreeing with a user's view, praising a plan or confirming a fear when a careful answer would push back. OpenAI's term for the April 2025 behavior was overly supportive but disingenuous. In June 2026 a coalition of state attorneys general, through New York's, asked OpenAI for records on model sycophancy, as reported by TechCrunch. [19][32]

9In the app today

What has already changed in ChatGPT, per OpenAI's notices.

Since Sept. 29, 2025Parental controls

A parent and a teen, 13 or older, can link accounts. The parent can then:

  • set quiet hours when ChatGPT can't be used
  • turn off voice mode, memory and image generation
  • opt the teen's chats out of model training
  • get an email, text or phone alert if a small team of specially trained people reviews signs of acute distress

OpenAI, Sept. 29, 2025 [27]

Since Aug. 18, 2026A separate version for teens

Users OpenAI's system estimates are under 18, or who give an age from 13 to 17, are placed in ChatGPT for Teens. Its rules say ChatGPT should not use romantic language, encourage emotional dependence, or imply that it has feelings or consciousness.

OpenAI, Aug. 18, 2026; Sept. 16, 2025 [26][33]

Since Oct. 27, 2025Crisis responses and breaks

Wider access to crisis hotlines; sensitive conversations re-routed to safer models; gentle reminders to take breaks during long sessions.

OpenAI, Oct. 27, 2025 [28]

Since Feb. 13, 2024Memory

ChatGPT can remember details from past chats. OpenAI's notice: You're in control of ChatGPT's memory. Parents can switch it off on a linked teen account.

OpenAI, Feb. 13, 2024; Sept. 29, 2025 [21][27]
