Ohio, the 2026 election

## At least $659 million spent on Ohio's 2026 races for Senate, governor and the U.S. House.

That is what the candidates' campaigns and the outside groups backing or attacking them have reported spending, to the Federal Election Commission and the Ohio Secretary of State, before Election Day on November 3. The Senate race accounts for $432.5 million of it, the race for governor $114.9 million, and the fifteen U.S. House races $111.6 million.

**How the total is counted, part by part, and what it leaves out**

Money spent in Ohio's 2026 races for U.S. Senate, governor and the U.S. House, as the spenders reported it: by the campaigns of the candidates on the November 3 ballot, and by the outside groups that reported spending for or against them, this election cycle, the May primary included. Each part is a filing total, through the date shown. For outside groups, only what each reported spending on these races is counted, so money one group gave another, such as MAGA Inc.'s $200 million to No Going Back PAC, is never counted twice. A campaign's spending is everything its committee paid out, which can include gifts to other committees.

**What the total leaves out**

- Spending after each part's date. Campaigns report again after the election; outside groups report each new buy within a day or two.
- Spending by the two campaigns for governor other than media and advertising: staff, travel, events and the rest are on their Ohio reports and are not summed here.
- Ohioans For Lower Costs, the committee through which the Democratic Governors Association funds its Ohio advertising: $9.4 million planned as of August (reported); its report due October 22 has not been read for this story. [[12]][[6]]
- The Republican Governors Association's advertising: "roughly $20 million" to date, it said, with no Ohio share given. [[6]]
- Ads by 501(c)(4) nonprofits that do not expressly call for a vote, which are reported to the FEC only in part or not at all.
- Candidates who lost the May primaries, and party committees' own spending.
- Every other race on Ohio's ballot: the other statewide offices, the Ohio Supreme Court, the General Assembly and local races. No money for a statewide ballot issue is in the record for this page.
- Vanessa Enoch's campaign (OH-08), which the FEC's processed summary still shows at $0; her e-filed report shows $38,917.76 raised this cycle. [[13]]

A second measure, kept apart and never added in: AdImpact's count of advertising aired and booked ahead. By that count the Senate race had drawn $423 million by Oct. 15 [[14]][[6]] and the governor's race $154.5 million by Oct. 8 [[6]], and AdImpact projects $749 million in political advertising across all Ohio races this cycle. The ad count

### Senate ads in Ohio: $106 million in 2022, $483.4 million in 2024, $423 million by October 15, 2026.

AdImpact counts the advertising in a race across broadcast, cable, satellite, radio, streaming and digital, including time booked ahead. Its 2026 count includes the candidates' own ads; $350M of the $423M came from outside groups. In 2024 the outside share was about 85 percent. [[16]] Figures are in the dollars of each year.

**Two ways of counting the same race**

AdImpact estimates the value of the airtime and digital space bought, including reservations. The Federal Election Commission counts what each committee reports it spent. The rules

### What the disclaimer laws require, and when the donors appear.

#### On the ad

Ads that support or oppose a federal candidate must say who paid. On television, an ad paid for by anyone other than the candidate must say aloud that the payer "is responsible for the content of this advertising" and show a written statement at least 4 percent of the screen's height for at least 4 seconds. [[91]] [[92]]

#### In Ohio

Ohio's disclaimer law, in its current version since September 30, 2025: anyone who pays for political advertising for or against an Ohio candidate or issue must identify itself on the ad; when a candidate or campaign pays, the line begins "paid for by"; broadcasters and printers may not run paid political ads without it. [[93]] The October 20 video in the governor's race first circulated without a "paid for" tag. [[23]]

#### Behind the name on the ad

A 501(c)(4) nonprofit may give to a super PAC without naming its own donors. One Nation gave Senate Leadership Fund $132.9 million this cycle. On air, the ad names Senate Leadership Fund. [[3]]

#### When the donors appear

No Going Back PAC, formed September 1, had reported $159.6 million in spending across its races by October 14 before any receipts were on file [[31]]; its first two reports, e-filed October 20 (the October monthly, covering September, and the pre-general, covering October 1 to 14), list $200 million from MAGA Inc. [[21]] Enough Promises Ohio PAC, formed September 29, had not disclosed its contributors by October 12 [[36]]; its pre-general report, e-filed October 22, lists one, Conservative Americans PAC, at $2.5 million. [[37]]

Ohio's pre-general report, due October 22, covers activity through October 14. From October 15 through Election Day, the campaign of the candidates for governor and lieutenant governor must also file a statement within two business days whenever one contributor's gifts in that period reach $10,000. [[26]] Money that passes through a state committee shows that committee's donors: Ohioans For Lower Costs shows $1 million from the IBEW. [[6]]
