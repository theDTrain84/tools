## Draft copy, fold by fold

**1. A levy raises the same dollars forever.** In 1976 Ohio passed House Bill 920. It says a voted levy collects the dollars it collected in its first year, no matter how much the houses under it appreciate. When Butler County's reappraisal raised values 37 percent in 2023 and 19 percent in 2026, Lakota's effective millage fell about 7 mills and its local revenue rose 3.5 percent. Costs rose faster. That is the machine: a district's income flattens while everything it buys gets more expensive, so it comes back to the ballot to stand still. [R.C. 319.301; LSC; Butler County Auditor]

**2. The court said so four times.** Between 1997 and 2002 the Ohio Supreme Court ruled, in DeRolph, that funding schools this heavily from local property tax violated the state constitution. It ordered a fix, then in 2003 ended its own jurisdiction and left the fix to the legislature. The second ruling named the problem "phantom revenue." [DeRolph I to IV, Ohio Supreme Court]

**3. The fix was built, then frozen.** The 2021 Fair School Funding Plan was the answer to DeRolph: a formula based on what it actually costs to educate a child, phased in over six years. The 2025 state budget finished the phase-in on 2022 cost figures and holds most districts at their 2020 or 2021 aid under a guarantee. State aid to traditional districts rises 0.4 percent this year and 1.3 percent next, while state voucher spending passed $1 billion. [HB 110, HB 33, HB 96; LSC Greenbook; Policy Matters Ohio's gap estimate, $2.86 billion]

**4. The pandemic money is gone.** Ohio districts received $7.14 billion in federal pandemic money between 2020 and 2024. It had to be committed by September 30, 2024. A lot of it paid for people. Troy City Schools' last $1.9 million landed in fiscal 2024; its renewal, passed every five years since 1996, failed in May 2026. [ESSER I, II, III; Ohio Auditor, Troy FY2024]

**5. In 2025 the legislature changed the rules.** Over the governor's veto, the House on July 21, 2025 and the Senate on October 1, 2025 ended new emergency, substitute and replacement levies from January 1, 2026, and capped growth at the 20-mill floor and on inside millage. Three more property-tax bills were signed December 19, 2025. A cap on how much cash a district may carry over, and a power for county commissions to cut levies, stayed on the table for an override before the end of 2026. [HB 96 veto overrides; HB 129, 186, 335]

**The result, this November.** 131 school tax issues are on Ohio ballots on November 3, 68 of them for new money. In May 2026, 24 of 66 passed. Three districts are in state fiscal emergency; Cincinnati is in fiscal caution; 48 more districts project negative cash by 2029. [Secretary of State list, Sept. 18; OEPI; ODEW 2026 report]

**Where Lakota sits.** Issue 2 is a bond: $223,483,492 over 37 years at 2.20 mills, with $77 million in state construction money on top, for buildings only. It cannot pay a teacher. It starts collecting in 2029, when a 2.23-mill bond from 2005 expires. Lakota's last operating levy was 2013, forecast to last five years and still funding the district thirteen years later. The $506 million package of November 2025 lost 61 to 39. [Butler County BOE certification Aug. 17, 2026; MFP funding page; Journal-News]

## What the candidates are saying (quotes, dated, sourced)
- Vivek Ramaswamy, September 3, 2026: "the property tax base is going to be rolled back in aggregate to where it was before the end of the pandemic"; a statewide model that "gradually weans us off of exclusive reliance on local property taxation" is "a long-run goal."
- Amy Acton, July 20, 2026 plan: raise the homestead exemption to $50,000; a $1,000 rebate when a bill rises more than 4 percent; "fully implement" the Fair School Funding Plan.
- Rep. David Thomas, on the funding plan: "Government should not take more than inflation without a vote of the people"; the plan is "a blank check."
- Husted and Brown: Husted helped create EdChoice; Brown has opposed vouchers (2022). Neither has a 2026 property-tax statement on the record. The page says that plainly.

## What the other side proposes, in their own documents
1. **Cap the cash districts carry.** 100 percent per the governor's working group (Sept. 30, 2025); 40 percent in the vetoed budget line; 30 percent in HB 335 as introduced. Troy's audited carryover was 40.1 percent. Lakota's current figure is unconfirmed (its forecast page is down).
2. **Consolidate the 611 districts.** "One per county" (Massie); combine declining-enrollment districts (the Buckeye Institute). No neutral body has costed it.
3. **Replace property tax with sales or income tax, or abolish it.** The Lincoln Institute (June 2026) priced replacement at a 13.36 percent sales tax or an 8.24 percent flat income tax; the state budget office says the income tax would have to quadruple. The abolition amendment missed the 2026 ballot and is aiming at 2027 with no replacement named.
4. **Vouchers as the growth line.** $394 million in 2020 to $1.09 billion in 2025, $2.44 billion budgeted for 2026 to 2027.
