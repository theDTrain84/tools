The "authorized through" year is how long the levy *may* run. It is where the claim that the 2005 bond "falls off in 2034" comes from. The payoff date is set by the bonds themselves, and those were refunded. The district's own post says the same: four series totaling $34.9 million, an average 2.28 mills needed 2026 to 2028, the 2005 series paid off in 2026, the 2020 series in 2027, the 2020A series in 2028.[7]

Verified **Ohio Auditor of State, Lakota LSD audited financial statements, FY2023, debt note.** The 2005-dated series (the 2006 Refunding) matures December 1, 2026. The 2020 Refunding matures December 1, 2027. The 2013 refunding of the 2005 bonds matures December 1, 2029. The only series maturing in 2034 is the 2014 school improvement bonds.[2] Updated by the **FY2025 audit** (opinion dated Dec 26, 2025): bonds outstanding $61.58M principal at June 30, 2025; the 2006 Refunding ($9.66M) matures Dec 1, 2026; the 2020 Refunding ($13.9M) Dec 1, 2027; the 2020A and 2020B refundings ($30.9M) carry no printed maturity; a 2016 refunding runs to Dec 1, 2033. Debt service by fiscal year: FY2026 $14.44M, FY2027 $14.20M, FY2028 $14.22M, FY2029 $12.42M (the last big maturity, paid from taxes collected in 2028), FY2030 $2.09M, then about $2.0M a year through FY2035. The audit's note: bonds are paid "from the debt service and permanent improvement funds." In FY2025 the debt-service fund paid $9.47M and the permanent-improvement fund $1.81M.[2]

Unknown Whether the smaller series after 2028 are paid from the voted bond levy or from the 2-mill permanent-improvement fund. **Where Lakota's dollars come from, and where they go ▾**

Two different questions are on the table this fall, and they are easy to run together. **A bond** borrows a fixed sum for buildings and is paid back over decades; the county sets the mills each year to cover the payment, so higher property values mean fewer mills, and the money can only be spent on the buildings in the plan. **An operating levy** pays for people and programs (teachers, buses, heat) out of the general fund, is set in mills, and under Ohio law those mills shrink as values rise so the dollars stay roughly flat. Issue 2 is a bond. The 3.6-mill figure in the district's forecast is an operating levy, on no ballot yet.[4]

**General fund, fiscal year 2026 (July 2025 to June 2026), actual, from the district's forecast filed with the state Aug 24, 2026.**[4]

**The cash balance, and why a levy is in the forecast.** The general fund ended FY2025 with $63.5 million after $51.2 million was transferred out of the fund that year; before that it held about $116 million. The forecast's notes explain only the extracurricular share of those transfers (about $3.7 million); where the rest went is not stated in the filing, which is a fair question for the treasurer. The forecast then spends more than it takes in each year and shows the balance falling below zero in FY2031 with no new money.[4]

The treasurer's own words on the levy in the forecast: "we have modeled a 3.6 mill levy to begin collecting in FY29 in order to bring the FY30 unreserved balance of the forecast above thirty (30) days of operating expenditures. This is an increase from the 3.4 mills modeled in the May 2025 submission." And the next sentence, which cuts the other way: "We believe the need for this levy could be mitigated, if not offset entirely, with operational savings from approval of the Master Facilities Plan by the district's taxpayers."[4] Both sentences are the district's. The page shows both.

The forecast also notes that new state law (House Bills 186 and 335) now caps how fast revenue from the district's 6.49 unvoted mills can grow, which is one reason the forecast turned down between May 2025 and August 2026.[4]

Figures are the district's, rounded to the nearest $0.1 million. The forecast is, in its own words, a snapshot in time; the next filing is due by the end of February 2027.

**How this is calculated, and how honest it is ▾**

Ohio taxes **35 percent** of a home's appraised value. West Chester Township homes in Lakota are tax district `M56`, about **47 effective mills** on a home; Liberty Township homes in Lakota (`D20`) are about 45 because the township rate is lower, and the school lines are identical. Your bill is that rate on 35 percent of the value you set. One mill is about `$35 per $100,000`.

**Pass / fail:** both ballot issues are things you pay for today, so the default is "pass." Flip one to "fail" and the bill and the pie drop by what that levy costs, with a line on what the community loses. Lakota's Issue 2 bond **replaces** the 2000 and 2005 bonds (2.28 mills today) and starts collecting in 2029, so "pass" barely moves the bill. The ten-year chart shows both futures on one calendar, stacked by bond, with the years the county sets from the debt schedule marked as estimates. Issue 6 is a countywide mental-health renewal at the same rate you pay now. The reappraisal toggle raises value, but Ohio law holds most voted taxes steady, so only a small part rises.

**Honest about the numbers:** the district rates and ballot figures are the county's TY2025 numbers and the certified ballot; the split across bodies is apportioned by millage until per-levy factors are loaded. Your treasurer's bill is always the record.
