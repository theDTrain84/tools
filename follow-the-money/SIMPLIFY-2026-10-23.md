## What the record says on the state's share

**Lakota (US Census Annual Survey of School System Finances, fiscal 1992 to 2024, src 13; this count treats the state's rollback and homestead credits as state money):**
- The state's share was 35.1 percent in 1992, peaked at 38.3 percent in 2003, was 37.3 percent in 2011, and fell in 11 of the 13 years after that, to 28.0 percent in 2023 and 28.8 percent in 2024.
- Local sources (property tax plus other local money) stayed between 56.5 percent (2011) and 67.8 percent (1995) in every year, and were 64.7 percent in 2024. His sense that the local share "has always been pretty much the same" matches the record. His sense that the state's slice has been shrinking also matches, from the 2003 peak and steadily since 2011.
- Federal money rose from 1 to 2 percent in the 1990s to 6.6 percent in 2024 (9.4 percent in 2023, with one-time pandemic aid).
- Per student, in 2025 dollars, state revenue fell 28 percent from fiscal 2005 to 2024 ($5,376 to $3,854), while local revenue rose 4 percent ($8,355 to $8,671). This is the page's existing arithmetic on Census figures with CPI-U.
- The state's own report (src 16, 37) puts Lakota's fiscal 2025 state share at 27.9 percent, against 37.0 percent for the average Ohio district. The district's FY27 forecast (src 14) puts the state budget at 30.9 percent of general-fund revenue and says, "We are considered a guarantee district and do not anticipate increases in state aid."
- Counting matters: the district prospectus (src 15) counts the state's homeowner credits as local and shows 75 / 21 / 4 for fiscal 2025.

**Statewide:** the research files hold no statewide state-share series. The only trend figure is Fair School Funding Plan WorkGroup testimony (Senate Finance, May 27, 2025, REPORTED, an advocacy source): the state's share of the formula falling from 42.35 percent in 2024 to a projected 32.2 percent by 2027. It is left off the page.
