# Editor read: "Why Ohio keeps voting on school levies" (follow-the-money, section h-levy, sources 43 to 61)

Cold read, October 21, 2026. Page not edited.

**Verdict: ready after must-fixes.** The numbers hold. Every figure I checked against a primary document matched. The must-fixes cover four statements that go further than the record, one house-rule breach, and two places where only one side's documents are on the page.

---

## MUST-FIX

**1. The lede and fold 1 state the freeze as universal. For 78 percent of Ohio districts it does not hold.**
- As it stands (lede): "In Ohio, a voted school levy collects the same dollars year after year, however much the homes under it gain in value. To raise more, a district has to ask voters again."
- Problem: LSC (src 44, p. 5) says a district at the 20-mill floor "gets full growth from reappraisal," and 477 of 611 districts were at the floor in 2024. Bond and emergency levies are voted levies too, and the reduction factor does not apply to them (LSC Table 1). The headline asks about Ohio, and the first sentence is false for most of Ohio. It is true for Lakota.
- Replace with: "In Ohio, a voted school operating levy collects about the same dollars each year from the homes already taxed, however much those homes gain in value, until a district's rate falls to a 20-mill floor. Lakota is above that floor. To raise more, a district like Lakota has to ask voters again." (cite 44, 17, 10)
- Same fix, fold 1 body. As it stands: "Each year the state sets a reduction factor for every voted levy so that it collects about what it collected the year before". Replace with: "Each year the state sets a reduction factor for each voted operating and permanent improvement levy so that it collects about what it collected the year before".
- Same fix, fold 1 headline and summary. As it stands: "A voted levy raises the same dollars every year" / "When home values rise, its rate falls instead." Replace with: "A voted operating levy raises about the same dollars every year" / "When home values rise, its effective rate falls."

**2. The DeRolph fold headline says the Court found the system unconstitutional four times. DeRolph III did not.**
- As it stands: headline "The Ohio Supreme Court ruled four times that school funding was unconstitutional"; body "September 2001 and December 11, 2002, DeRolph III and IV. The fourth ruling held that 'the current school funding system is unconstitutional.'"
- Problem: the LSC brief's own body (src 46, p. 4) says that in DeRolph III the Court "found that most components of the new system complied" and held that with certain revisions "the new system would be constitutional." DeRolph IV then vacated III. The brief's one-paragraph summary is looser than its body, and the page took the summary.
- Replace headline with: "The Ohio Supreme Court ruled three times that school funding was unconstitutional"
- Replace the body line with: "**September 2001, DeRolph III.** The Court found most of the new system complied and said it would be constitutional once the state made further changes, including full funding of parity aid by July 1, 2003. **December 11, 2002, DeRolph IV.** The Court vacated that order and held that 'the current school funding system is unconstitutional.'" (cite 18, 46)

**3. "Two vetoed items remain open." Many more remain open.**
- As it stands: "Two vetoed items remain open to an override vote through December 31, 2026: county budget commission power to cut voted levies, and a cap on the cash a district may carry over, 40 percent of a year's spending in the vetoed line."
- Problem: the House Journal for July 21, 2025 (src 54) leaves every other item pending ("remaining line item vetoes 1, 2, 3, ... 55, ... 65, and 67 be left pending"). Item 65, the 20-mill floor calculation, is a third school property-tax item still open (research file, sec. 1).
- Replace with: "Other vetoed items stay open to an override vote through December 31, 2026. Two of them bear on school levies: county budget commission power to cut voted levies (item 55), and a rule letting those commissions cut a district's levies when its carryover tops 40 percent of a year's spending (item 63)." (cite 55, 54)

**4. Troy's 40.1 percent sits next to the 40 percent trigger, but it is a different measure.**
- As it stands: "Troy's audited unassigned general fund balance at June 30, 2024 was 40.1 percent of that year's spending."
- Problem: an audited unassigned fund balance (a GAAP figure) is not the carryover the vetoed line measured. Set beside the trigger, the sentence tells the reader Troy would have been caught by it. No document says so.
- Replace with: cut the sentence. If he wants to keep it: "Troy's audit puts its unassigned general fund balance at June 30, 2024 at 40.1 percent of that year's spending, an accounting measure close to the vetoed line's carryover figure but a different one." (cite 50)

**5. House rule: "not X" contrast in the bond box.**
- As it stands: "The money pays for the buildings and the debt, not teachers or daily costs."
- Replace with: "The money can be spent only on the buildings in the plan and the debt on them." (cite 6, 44)

**6. Fold 5 gives what the override did but not why. Only one side's reasoning is on the page.**
- As it stands: fold 5 reports the vote and the effects. The spread's account of why levies keep coming (HB 920, DeRolph, the formula, ESSER, the 2025 limits) is the school side's account. The legislature's stated reason for item 66 appears nowhere except in candidate quotes further down. The levy-tool rule is "both sides' documents on the page."
- Add after the first paragraph of fold 5: "The House Republican release on the Senate vote said the provision 'ensures taxpayers have more control over their property tax bills.' The House Democratic leader's release on July 21 called the override 'not real property tax relief.'" (sources: ohiohouse.gov release of Oct. 1, 2025, Rep. Adam Mathews, https://ohiohouse.gov/news/republican/house-senate-override-veto-on-key-property-tax-reform-138118 ; Leader Isaacsohn release of July 21, 2025, https://ohiohouse.gov/news/democratic/leader-isaacsohn-republicans-veto-override-leaves-local-communities-holding-the-bag-still-no-real-property-relief-for-majority-of-ohioans-135851 . Both are VERIFIED in the candidates research file, sec. 2 and 8. Add them to src 54 or as a new source.)

**7. Fold 4 picks the lowest number for its summary line and sets it beside vouchers, with no Republican document.**
- As it stands (summary): "Statewide, aid to traditional districts rises 0.4 percent in fiscal 2026." Body: 0.4 and 1.3 percent, then community and STEM schools at 4.8 and 5.0, then voucher growth.
- Problem: the summary takes the 0.4 and leaves off fiscal 2027. The body lines up traditional aid, charter aid and vouchers the way one side argues it, and the budget's supporters' figure is left out, even though that figure is already in a cited source (src 57, Gross and Hall releases).
- Replace summary with: "Statewide, aid to traditional districts rises 0.4 percent in fiscal 2026 and 1.3 percent in 2027."
- Add after the Greenbook sentence: "Republican House members' releases on the final budget put it at 'nearly $700 million more for public education over the biennium than they received in FY25.'" (cite 57)

## SHOULD-FIX

1. **"median home value rose 37 percent ... and 19 percent."** The sources measure the median *increase* ("The median increase in valuations"; "values are increasing countywide by a median of 19 percent"). Reword: "Butler County values rose by a median of 37 percent in the 2023 update and 19 percent in the 2026 reappraisal." Chart key: "Median value increase, Butler County."
2. **"the rate on each voted operating levy falls"**: say "effective rate". The statute says the voted rate is not reduced; the effective rate is (R.C. 319.301; Lakota forecast: voted 64.14 mills, Class I effective 23.23).
3. **"passed in 1976"** cites 43 and 44, and neither says 1976. Src 17 (LSC 2019, p. 70) does: "H.B. 920 is a tax policy that was enacted in 1976." Add 17 to that sentence.
4. **Why Troy and Talawanda?** The ESSER table never says why these two districts. Add a line: "Two nearby districts, for comparison: Troy City in Miami County and Talawanda in Butler County, both of which put operating levies to voters since the money ran out." (Talawanda's 2022 vote predates the end of ESSER, so adjust: "both with operating levies on recent ballots.")
5. **Plain words.** Not defined in this section for a parent: *mills* (one mill is $1 per $1,000 of taxable value), *effective rate*, *replacement / substitute / emergency levy*, *renewal with an increase*, *fiscal caution, watch or emergency*, *Cupp-Patterson* (the plan's two sponsors; source needed for their names and parties before adding). One clause each in fold 5 and the formula-names panel.
6. **"Yes, 61: all Republicans."** This can be read as "all Republicans voted yes," which is false (Oelslager no, 3 not recorded). Replace with "Yes, 61: all of them Republicans."
7. **Per-candidate "Voted yes on the July 21, 2025 override" lines.** The panel above already says every Republican incumbent in these districts voted yes. Repeating it six times singles out the one party-line vote of the year. The same members also voted for HB 129, 186 and 335, which passed with many Democratic votes. Either cut the repeated lines (the panel carries it) or list all four votes for each incumbent.
8. **"131 school tax issues are on Ohio ballots."** The figure is one outside tally of the Secretary of State's list as of Sept. 18. Write: "As of September 18, the Secretary of State's list carried 131 school tax issues for November 3, 68 for new money, by one outside count." WVXU (Oct. 7, 2026, "more than 130 education issues") is a second source worth adding to src 61.
9. **Item 66's carryover clause is missing.** Per the Gross release of July 22, 2025 (src 58), item 66 also bars a new current-expense levy when prior-year carryover exceeds 100 percent of expenses. That belongs beside "Lakota's current carryover percentage: Unknown."
10. **"held to its first-year dollars"** (operating box). Write "held to about the dollars it first raised, until the district reaches the 20-mill floor."
11. **The 2013 levy's 2.0 permanent improvement mills.** Src 21 (the 2013 results PDF) confirms "by 288 votes" (13,645 to 13,357) but not the mill split. The 3.50 operating mills are in the FY27 forecast (src 14). Cite the source for 2.0 PI, or drop the split.
12. **The vote-and-names panel holds two ideas** (one override vote; two names for the formula). Splitting it would keep one idea per screen.
13. **The candidates fold's scope.** "the 8th District's Butler, Preble, Darke and Miami County seats": say "8th Congressional District," and give the reader one reason the fold reaches past Lakota's two districts, or limit it to 45 and 46.
14. **Voucher line context.** Add that House Bill 33 (2023) opened EdChoice Expansion to all incomes (REPORTED, Ideastream, June 27, 2025), so the jump from $394.2 million to $1.09 billion has its cause beside it.
15. **Bottom chart panel stops at tax year 2024.** The FY27 forecast (src 14) gives a Class I effective rate of 23.23 mills for tax year 2025. Optional: add it.
16. **Fold 1 bond sentence** ("Their rate is set each year to collect that year's debt payment") cites 44, which says only that bonds are not subject to the factor. Cite R.C. 5705 or the district resolution (src 6) for the annual reset.
