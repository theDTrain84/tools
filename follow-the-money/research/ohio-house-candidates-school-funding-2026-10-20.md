Prepared Oct. 20, 2026, for the Follow the Money series. Companion to ohio-school-levies-2026-10-20.md.

Tags: VERIFIED = the primary document or official page was opened and read. REPORTED = a named outlet's report; the page was opened unless noted. UNCONFIRMED = listed in section 9 and not to be used as fact without a second source.

The author's question, verbatim: "in Ohio's 2026 state House races, school funding and property tax look like a dividing line between Republicans and Democrats. Find the candidates' actual positions, race by race, on the record."

Two corrections to the brief before anything else. (1) Ohio House District 31 is not in Hamilton County; it is Summit County (Cuyahoga Falls, Barberton, Norton, Bath, Richfield), the seat Bill Roemer holds. It is outside the 8th Congressional District and is covered here only as a note. (2) Ohio Senate districts 4 and 8 are even-numbered and are not on the 2026 ballot; the Senate seats that overlap the 8th Congressional District and are up this year are the 5th and the 7th. Details in sections 1 and 7.

## 2. The hardest record: the 2025 roll calls

### HB 96 veto override, House, July 21, 2025 (Item 66, school district levy restrictions)

- VERIFIED. House Journal, Monday, July 21, 2025, Sixty-Third Day, pages 1097 to 1099. The House took up one item: "The House proceeded to the consideration of the item in Am. Sub. H. B. No. 96 vetoed by the Governor. ITEM NUMBER 66." "The question being, 'Shall the item pass notwithstanding the objections of the Governor?' The yeas and nays were taken and resulted, yeas 61, nays 28." (Dash in the Journal rendered as a comma.) Then: "Representative Manning moved that remaining line item vetoes 1, 2, 3, ... 55, ... 65, and 67 be left pending. The motion was agreed to without objection." So items 55 (county budget commission authority) and 65 (20-mill floor calculation) were not voted on July 21; only item 66 was. https://search-prod.lis.state.oh.us/api/v2/general_assembly_136/journals/journal_h_20252107/pdf
- VERIFIED. Yeas (61), as printed in the Journal: Barhorst, Bird, Callender, Claggett, Click, Craig, Creech, Daniels, Dean, Deeter, Demetriou, Dovilla, Ferguson, Fischer, Fowler Arthur, Ghanbari, Gross, Hall T., Hiner, Holmes, Hoops, John, Johnson, King, Kishman, Klopfenstein, Lampton, LaRe, Lear, Lorenz, Manning, Mathews A., Mathews T., McClain, Miller K., Miller M., Moore, Mullins, Newman, Odioso, Peterson, Pizzulli, Plummer, Richardson, Ritter, Robb Blasdel, Roemer, Salvo, Santucci, Schmidt, Stewart, Swearingen, Teska, Thomas D., Thomas J., White A., Williams, Willis, Workman, Young, Huffman.
- VERIFIED. Nays (28): Brennan, Brent, Brewer, Cockley, Denson, Grim, Hall D., Humphrey, Isaacsohn, Jarrells, Lawson-Rowe, Lett, Mohamed, Oelslager, Piccolantonio, Rader, Robinson, Rogers, Sigrist, Sims, Somani, Sweeney, Synenberg, Thomas C., Tims, Troy, Upchurch, White E. Every Democrat present voted no; one Republican (Oelslager) voted no; ten members were not recorded.
- VERIFIED. The same tally and names appear on the Ohio House bill page (7/21/2025, Passed, 61 to 28). https://ohiohouse.gov/legislation/136/hb96/votes
- VERIFIED. What item 66 does, per the House Republican release of July 22, 2025 (Rep. Gross's office): ends local governments' ability to place new replacement levies on the ballot; ends school districts' ability to put fixed-sum emergency or substitute levies, renewals with increases, and combined income tax and fixed-sum property tax levies on the ballot; bars a district from asking for a new current expense levy if its prior-year carryover exceeds 100 percent of expenses; requires a public meeting before a district changes the purpose of inside millage. https://ohiohouse.gov/members/jennifer-gross/news/rep-gross-votes-to-override-veto-spurring-property-tax-relief-for-ohioans-135911
- VERIFIED. The Senate completed the override on Oct. 1, 2025 (House Republican release of that date, Rep. Adam Mathews: "I am grateful to our Senate colleagues for voting to override the veto."). https://ohiohouse.gov/news/republican/house-senate-override-veto-on-key-property-tax-reform-138118 REPORTED: the Senate vote was 21 to 11 (WYSU, Oct. 1, 2025; Squire Patton Boggs; Ohio School Boards Association, Oct. 12, 2025). https://wysu.org/ohio-news/2025-10-01/ohio-senate-overrides-veto-eliminating-some-kinds-of-tax-levies
- VERIFIED. The Ohio School Boards Association's list of the education items DeWine vetoed on June 30, 2025: Item 27 (non-chartered ESA and tax credits), 51 (STRS and SERS contributions), 53 (partisan school board elections), 55 (county budget commission authority, partial), 63 (40 percent cash balance carryover), 65 (20-mill floor calculation including fixed-sum levies), 66 (school district property tax restrictions, elimination of fixed-sum levies). https://www.ohioschoolboards.org/node/726658

### HB 96 itself (the budget that set school funding), House votes

- VERIFIED. April 9, 2025, House version, Passed 60 to 39. June 25, 2025, final version, Passed 59 to 38. Roll calls on the House bill page. https://ohiohouse.gov/legislation/136/hb96/votes
- VERIFIED. In-district members on the June 25 final budget: Yea: Abrams, Creech, Gross, T. Hall, King, Newman, Odioso, Plummer, Roemer. Nay: Baker, Brownlee, Isaacsohn, C. Thomas. Not recorded: Mullins. On April 9: Gross voted Nay on the House version; Mullins voted Yea; the others as on June 25.
- REPORTED. What the House version did to school funding (Ohio Capital Journal, April 14, 2025, and WYSU, April 9, 2025): the House dropped the Fair School Funding Plan formula's phase-in and used 2022 base-cost inputs; it added a provision requiring county budget commissions to lower school property tax rates where district cash reserves exceeded 30 percent of operating costs (the figure became 40 percent in the final bill and was vetoed as Item 63). https://ohiocapitaljournal.com/2025/04/14/public-schools-could-have-less-property-tax-help-under-ohio-house-education-budget/ and https://wysu.org/ohio-news/2025-04-09/ohio-house-passes-changes-to-k-12-public-school-funding-in-two-year-operating-budget

### What became law

- REPORTED. Gov. DeWine signed HB 124, HB 129, HB 186, HB 309 and HB 335 on Dec. 19, 2025 (Signal Ohio, Andrew Tobias). DeWine: "These bills bring about meaningful, impactful tax relief that brings about clarity." The package caps automatic increases driven by rising values, sets aside $410 million for schools, changes the 20-mill floor calculation, and gives county budget commissions more authority to reduce voter-approved levies. https://signalohio.org/dewine-signs-bills-overhauling-property-taxes-cutting-taxes-by-billions/
- REPORTED. WYSO (Dec. 19, 2025): the state will pay schools from unused sales-tax holiday funding, "$306 million in fiscal year 2026, 105 million in fiscal year 2027." https://www.wyso.org/2025-12-19/dewine-signs-package-of-bills-aimed-at-lowering-property-tax-bill-spikes-in-ohio
- REPORTED. The citizen amendment to abolish property taxes did not make the 2026 ballot; the campaign (AxOHTax, Brian Massie) announced June 5, 2026 it would aim for November 2027 after reporting about 305,000 signatures against 413,488 needed (Ohio Capital Journal, June 9, 2026; 13abc, June 5, 2026). https://ohiocapitaljournal.com/2026/06/09/constitutional-amendment-to-eliminate-property-taxes-in-ohio-will-not-appear-on-november-ballot/
- REPORTED. Ohio Manufacturers' Association (Aug. 15, 2025): "Republican State Reps. Jennifer Gross and Tex Fischer, along with Lake County Commissioner John Plecnik" signed the abolition petition. No quotes on the page. https://www.ohiomfg.com/our-communities/property-tax-abolishment-gains-favor-in-ohio-statehouse/

## 10. Read: does the record support "a dividing line between the parties"?

The record supports the observation on one vote and complicates it on the rest. The cleanest party line of 2025 is the July 21 House override of Item 66 (the end of fixed-sum emergency and substitute school levies and of replacement levies): 61 Republicans yes, every Democrat present no, one Republican (Oelslager) no. Every Republican incumbent in the districts covered here (Gross, Hall, Mullins, Odioso, Creech, King, Newman, Plummer) voted yes; Isaacsohn voted no; Baker, Brownlee and Abrams were not recorded. The words divide the same way: Republican incumbents describe the 2025 bills as "relief" and "caps on unvoted increases" (King: "capping unvoted tax increases to inflation"; Thomas: "Currently there is no limit to what schools can take from the property owner. That is over"), and Democrats describe property tax as the symptom of state underfunding (Isaacsohn: "The state can't keep putting school funding on the backs of local homeowners, it needs to pay the fair share"; Bradner: "about 51% of your property tax bill goes to fund local schools, when that number should be closer to 25%, if the state were meeting its obligation"; Jacob: "Ohio ranks 45th in the nation in the state share of public school funding"). The Fair School Funding Plan appears by name on the Democratic side only (Price, Hornbacker, Childrey, Baker's newsletter, Acton's plan) and on no Republican page or release in this file; the Republican releases call the enacted formula "the final phase of the Cupp-Patterson funding model" with "nearly $700 million more for public education." The complication is the follow-up bills: HB 129 passed 81 to 8 with Baker, Isaacsohn, Russo and Sweeney voting yes; HB 186 and HB 335 passed with Baker and Brownlee voting yes and Brownlee issuing a release calling them "necessary first steps," while the no votes were a block of mostly urban Democrats plus Isaacsohn. Two Republican challengers (Aichholz, Cole) say nothing about property tax and promise "increased funding for education" or public schools that vouchers "must not come at the expense of," and one Republican Senate candidate (Haines) proposes to "completely decouple property taxes from public education funding," which is closer to the Democratic diagnosis than to his own caucus's bills. The silences are themselves a pattern: all three Republican House incumbents in Preble, Darke and Miami left the League of Women Voters questionnaire blank, Mullins has no statement on either subject anywhere, and Odioso, vice chair of the Education Committee, has none in his own words; every Democratic challenger in the file has a written position on school funding, and all but Rasheed, Haire and Wagner have a specific property tax plank. On the abolition amendment the parties do not divide: Republican officeholders (DeWine, Cirino, Haines) and Democrats (Isaacsohn, Childrey, Harris, Price) both oppose it; the only in-district candidate backing it is the Libertarian Umbaugh, and the only Republican tied to it is Gross, by signature, REPORTED.

Count for the summary: 11 House districts covered, 10 contested (24 is uncontested). Both major-party candidates have a position in their own words on school funding or property tax in 6 (45, 46, 29, 40, 84, 80). In 2 (30, 47) the Republican incumbent has no words on either subject and only a roll call (Odioso, Mullins). In 2 (27, 28) the Republican challenger has a schools line ("increased funding for education"; vouchers "must not come at the expense of strong, accountable public schools") but nothing on property tax. Every Democrat in all 10 has a written position on school funding. Both Senate races (5 and 7) have both candidates on the record in their own words.
