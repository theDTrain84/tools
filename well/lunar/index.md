The year's sky

### What the sky is doing in 2026.

Full moons, showers, planets, eclipses, and the turning points of the year. Upcoming first.

1. **Oct 21 · Orionids** (Meteors). Halley's debris again. Fast meteors and bright fireballs. They seem to fly from Orion's club. What falls is what was released.
2. **Oct 24 · Mercury retrograde begins** (Astrology). Read the contract three times before signing.
3. **Oct 26 · Hunter's Moon** (Full moon). Leaves falling, fields cleared, what remains in view. What becomes visible when you stop adding new things?
4. **Oct 31 · Samhain** (Wheel of the year). The last cross-quarter festival of the year. A night to remember the dead. The boundary is thin tonight, and it is intimate. The ones you have lost are close.
5. **Nov 4 · Taurid fireballs** (Meteors). About 5 an hour, known for bright fireballs. Two streams, one from an asteroid and one from a comet, meet in the same sky. Collaboration is built into the universe.
6. **Nov 13 · Mercury retrograde ends** (Astrology). Clear to proceed.
7. **Nov 17 · Leonids** (Meteors). Usually 10 to 15 an hour. In 1966, thousands a minute for a few minutes. The Leonids storm on a 33-year cycle. The last storm was 2001. Patience is readiness.
8. **Nov 24 · Beaver Supermoon, Kartik Purnima** (Full moon). A supermoon on Kartik Purnima, which celebrates Shiva's victory over Tripurasura. A moon of preparation, with a light hard to ignore.
9. **Dec 13 · Geminids** (Meteors). Up to 150 an hour, in many colors. The best shower of winter. Most showers come from comets. The Geminids come from the asteroid 3200 Phaethon. Even rock, given time, becomes light.
10. **Dec 21 · December solstice** (Solstice). The shortest day. Winter begins in the north. The darkest day is the moment the light starts back. That is physics, and it is theology.
11. **Dec 24 · Cold Supermoon** (Full moon). Third supermoon of the year. The Cree call it the Frost Exploding Trees Moon. The Cold Moon rides highest and stays longest. The longest nights hold the most moonlight.

**Earlier in the year**

1. **Jan 3 · Wolf Supermoon** (Full moon). First of three supermoons this year. The pack draws close in the deepest cold. Who is yours?
2. **Jan 3 · Quadrantids** (Meteors). Up to 40 meteors an hour at the peak, Jan 3 to 4, washed out by the bright moon. Their parent, 2003 EH1, may be an extinct comet. Even gone, it still sends light.
3. **Jan 10 · Jupiter at opposition** (Planet). Jupiter at its closest and brightest, up all night. Binoculars show the four Galilean moons. Jupiter gives off more heat than it receives, warming itself by slowly contracting. Some things make warmth by becoming more themselves.
4. **Feb 1 · Snow Moon** (Full moon). The heaviest snows fall now. The land is composting. Spring's insight is being prepared in the dark soil of not-yet-knowing.
5. **Feb 1 · Imbolc** (Wheel of the year). Cross-quarter day between the winter solstice and the spring equinox. The first signs of spring.
6. **Feb 25 · Mercury retrograde begins** (Astrology). A season for review: back up, reconnect, reread.
7. **Mar 3 · Total lunar eclipse, Worm Moon** (Eclipse). Totality is best seen from western North America, East Asia and the Pacific. It falls on the Lantern Festival. In the old story, a town lit lanterns so the Jade Emperor would think it already burning, and it was spared. Sometimes celebration is the rescue.
8. **Mar 20 · March equinox** (Equinox). Day and night nearly equal. Spring begins in the north. For one day the whole planet shares the same measure of light. What would it mean to live that way?
9. **Mar 21 · Mercury retrograde ends** (Astrology). Communication clears. Move forward.
10. **Apr 2 · Pink Moon** (Full moon). Named for wild phlox, the first pink flowers of spring. This moon asks what you are willing to put in the ground.
11. **Apr 3 · Mercury at its best** (Planet). Greatest western elongation. Look low in the east before sunrise.
12. **Apr 22 · Lyrids** (Meteors). 10 to 15 an hour, with rare surges near 100. Dark skies this year. They radiate from near Vega in Lyra, the lyre of Orpheus, whose music moved stones. Fragments of something once whole.
13. **May 1 · Flower Moon, Vesak** (Full moon). Many communities keep the Buddha's birth, awakening and passing on this full moon. Vesak holds birth, awakening and death in a single day. The flower and the compost are one event seen at different moments.
14. **May 1 · Beltane** (Wheel of the year). Fire festival of growth, halfway from the spring equinox to the summer solstice.
15. **May 6 · Eta Aquariids** (Meteors). Up to 60 an hour in the south, 30 in the north. Debris from Halley's Comet. Halley saw that the comet returning every 76 years was one thing. Pattern recognition as an act of faith.
16. **May 31 · Blue Moon** (Full moon). The second full moon in May. A blue moon is surplus time. How will you honor it?
17. **Jun 21 · June solstice** (Solstice). The longest day of the year. Summer begins in the north. At the peak of light, the days begin to shorten. The fullest moment carries its own release.
18. **Jun 29 · Strawberry Moon** (Full moon). Named for the brief season of wild strawberries. What is sweetest does not last. Savor it.
19. **Jun 29 · Mercury retrograde begins** (Astrology). Review before you launch.
20. **Jul 6 · Earth at aphelion** (Planet). Earth at its farthest from the sun, in the middle of northern summer. Seasons come from tilt more than distance. Orientation matters most.
21. **Jul 24 · Mercury retrograde ends** (Astrology). Communication clears again.
22. **Jul 28 · Delta Aquariids** (Meteors). 15 to 20 an hour, steady through late July with no sharp peak. Some gifts keep showing up without announcing themselves.
23. **Jul 29 · Buck Moon** (Full moon). Named for new antlers. Also the Thunder Moon and the Hay Moon. Antlers can grow an inch a day. The growth costs bone and blood, and then it crowns.
24. **Aug 1 · Lughnasadh** (Wheel of the year). Cross-quarter day that opens the harvest.
25. **Aug 12 · Total solar eclipse** (Eclipse). Totality crosses Greenland, Iceland and northern Spain. For a few minutes the moon steps in front of the sun and the corona appears. It was there all along, hidden by the brightness.
26. **Aug 12 · Perseids** (Meteors). Up to 60 an hour under a new moon. The best shower of the year. Debris from Comet Swift-Tuttle, crossing our path every August. We stand in the dark and watch old dust become light.
27. **Aug 15 · Venus, the evening star** (Planet). Greatest eastern elongation. Bright in the west after sunset. Venus has phases like the moon. Its full phase happens behind the sun, out of sight. Some fullness can only be inferred.
28. **Aug 28 · Sturgeon Moon** (Full moon). Named for the ancient fish of the Great Lakes. A deep partial lunar eclipse this night. The sturgeon finds what sustains it along the bottom. What sustains you?
29. **Sep 23 · September equinox** (Equinox). Day and night nearly equal again. Autumn begins in the north. Balance returns for a day. Equilibrium is a passage.
30. **Sep 26 · Harvest Moon** (Full moon). The full moon nearest the equinox, rising only minutes later each night. The only moon named for its usefulness. It stays longer and gives light when light is needed most.
31. **Oct 4 · Saturn at opposition** (Planet). Saturn at its closest and brightest. The rings show in a small telescope. Saturn's rings may be young by the sky's measure. Even the icons of the cosmos arrived at some point.
32. **Oct 7 · Draconids** (Meteors). Radiant near the head of Draco. Watch in the early evening. In nearly every culture the dragon guards a treasure or a threshold. What are you guarding?
